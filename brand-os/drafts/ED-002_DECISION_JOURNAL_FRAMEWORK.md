# ED-002 — Decision Journals: Separating Thesis Quality, Sizing Quality and Outcome

Status: INTERNAL DRAFT — NOT APPROVED FOR EXTERNAL MARKETING  
Editorial inventory ID: ED-002  
Pillar: Research in practice  
Claim posture: Educational/process only; no performance, AUM, superiority, client, composite or assurance claims.

## Editorial purpose

Develop a durable RCG point of view around a simple problem: investment outcomes alone are a poor audit trail for decision quality. A profitable position can originate from weak reasoning; a losing position can originate from a sound, probabilistic process. A useful journal therefore has to preserve what was knowable at the time and evaluate thesis, implementation and outcome separately.

This draft is intentionally strategy-agnostic. It does not represent that RCG currently captures every field below. Before external use, any statement describing RCG's actual operating practice must be verified against contemporaneous records.

## Core editorial thesis

A decision journal should make four questions independently answerable:

1. **What did we believe?** — the thesis and causal mechanism.
2. **What did we risk?** — sizing, invalidation and portfolio interaction.
3. **What happened?** — the observed path and realized outcome.
4. **What did we learn?** — whether the original reasoning, implementation or subsequent adaptation deserves revision.

Collapsing these questions into "made money / lost money" creates hindsight bias and makes process improvement harder.

## Minimum contemporaneous record

Capture before or at the decision point:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Decision timestamp / as-of | Freezes the information set |
| Instrument / exposure | Defines what is actually being expressed |
| Thesis in one sentence | Forces falsifiable clarity |
| Causal mechanism | Explains why the expected effect should occur |
| Key evidence | Identifies the observations carrying the thesis |
| Material contrary evidence | Prevents one-sided evidence collection |
| Base / bull / bear cases | Makes uncertainty explicit |
| Expected horizon | Separates thesis timing from eventual outcome |
| Invalidation conditions | Defines what would change the view |
| Initial size and risk budget | Records implementation rather than reconstructing it |
| Portfolio interaction | Captures concentration, correlation and offsetting exposures |
| Known event/calendar risks | Preserves foreseeable path dependency |
| Confidence | Records belief strength without converting it into certainty |
| Next review trigger | Creates an explicit re-evaluation point |

The journal should preserve source identifiers and as-of dates for material evidence. A later edit should append rather than overwrite the original thesis.

## Three-axis review

### 1. Thesis quality

Evaluate the reasoning using only information reasonably available at the original decision time.

Questions:
- Was the causal mechanism coherent?
- Were important disconfirming facts considered?
- Were assumptions observable or testable?
- Was uncertainty represented rather than suppressed?
- Did the thesis specify conditions that could invalidate it?

Do not grade thesis quality from P&L.

### 2. Sizing and implementation quality

Evaluate whether the exposure was appropriate for the stated uncertainty and portfolio context.

Questions:
- Was size consistent with confidence and downside?
- Did the position create unintended concentration?
- Was liquidity compatible with the intended horizon and exit conditions?
- Were known event risks reflected in sizing?
- Did implementation drift away from the documented thesis?

A correct directional view can still be poorly implemented.

### 3. Outcome

Record what occurred without retroactively changing the first two grades.

Questions:
- Which scenario actually occurred?
- Which assumptions were confirmed or falsified?
- Was the result driven by the documented mechanism or by an unrelated event?
- Did timing differ materially from the expected horizon?
- What portion of the outcome is reasonably attributable to decision quality versus path dependency or luck?

Outcome is evidence for learning, not a substitute for the original record.

## Internal review matrix

| Thesis | Sizing | Outcome | Review interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sound | Sound | Favorable | Process and outcome aligned; still test for luck and omitted risks |
| Sound | Sound | Unfavorable | Examine scenario probability and new information before changing process |
| Sound | Weak | Favorable | Do not let profit excuse implementation error |
| Sound | Weak | Unfavorable | Separate thesis lesson from sizing lesson |
| Weak | Sound | Favorable | Treat as a warning, not validation |
| Weak | Sound | Unfavorable | Diagnose research failure without inventing hindsight certainty |
| Weak | Weak | Favorable | Highest risk of learning the wrong lesson |
| Weak | Weak | Unfavorable | Multiple failure modes; remediate independently |

"Sound" and "weak" are review conclusions, not mechanical scores. A reviewer should document the evidence supporting the classification.

## Update protocol

When facts change, append a dated update containing:
- new observation;
- source and as-of date;
- whether it confirms, weakens or invalidates an original assumption;
- resulting action: hold, resize, hedge, exit or no action;
- reason the action follows from the updated information.

Do not rewrite the initial thesis to make it resemble the eventual path.

## Postmortem discipline

A postmortem should distinguish:
- **forecast error** — an expected state did not occur;
- **mechanism error** — the causal explanation was wrong;
- **timing error** — the mechanism may have been right but the horizon was wrong;
- **sizing error** — exposure was inconsistent with uncertainty or downside;
- **implementation error** — execution departed from the intended expression;
- **information error** — a material fact/source was missing, stale or misunderstood;
- **adaptation error** — new evidence arrived but the decision was not appropriately revisited;
- **luck/path dependency** — the outcome was materially driven by factors outside the documented thesis.

Multiple classifications may apply. The objective is not to assign blame; it is to identify which part of the process should change.

## Anti-cherry-picking rule for any future external example

Do not select an example because its outcome makes RCG look prescient. A publication candidate should use a predeclared selection rule such as:
- a fixed time window;
- every decision meeting a neutral category definition;
- a randomly selected eligible case; or
- paired favorable/unfavorable outcomes illustrating the same process issue.

The evidence packet must preserve the eligible population and selection method. Any example involving a security, position, recommendation or performance implication requires controlled-claim review.

## Evidence gates before external use

External prose may say that a decision journal **can** or **should** contain the framework above without asserting RCG implementation.

Before saying "RCG does/uses/records/reviews" any element, require:
1. dated operating evidence showing the practice;
2. enough coverage to avoid presenting an aspirational template as established routine;
3. confidential-data review and anonymization;
4. Claim Registry mapping for any controlled factual representation;
5. compliance review if an example could imply performance, recommendation quality or investment superiority.

## Deliberate exclusions

This draft makes no claim about:
- RCG investment performance or historical results;
- AUM, clients or account counts;
- composites or proxy-account performance;
- regulatory status;
- audited, verified or proven results;
- specific securities, recommendations or positions;
- superiority versus other managers;
- the current implementation completeness of any RCG system.
